Open cases

Five things I want to build next. Each one names the person it is for — “everyone” is not an answer.

Case 01

The Revision Timetable

Who it's for
Classmates in the two weeks before exams.
Why it exists
A revision plan survives in your head until about the third day. Then it quietly disappears.

How it works

  1. Type a course name and its exam date into a short form.
  2. The page lays every course across one week, heaviest first.
  3. Ticks are saved in the browser, so the plan survives a refresh.

What it needs

  • Form
  • Date handling
  • localStorage

Case 02

The Fridge Ledger

Who it's for
Flatmates who share one fridge and four opinions about whose milk it is.
Why it exists
Food expires while nobody is looking. The label is always too small or already gone.

How it works

  1. Add an item, its owner and the day it goes off.
  2. The list sorts itself by expiry date.
  3. Anything with three days left turns red — the one colour nobody ignores.

What it needs

  • Form
  • Array sort
  • localStorage

Case 03

The Lost & Found Board

Who it's for
Anyone on campus who has lost a card, a bottle or a bike light.
Why it exists
Lost things live in group chats and vanish in an hour. A board can hold a photo and a place.

How it works

  1. Post a card with a photo, a place and a contact address.
  2. Filter the board by kind: cards, keys, bottles, everything else.
  3. Mark a card as returned and it leaves the board.

What it needs

  • File preview
  • Filtering
  • Cards

Case 04

The Reading Queue

Who it's for
Me, during the semester, when the reading pile grows faster than the week.
Why it exists
Six tabs and a bookmark folder is not a plan. A queue with a number on it is.

How it works

  1. Add a reading with a link and a page count.
  2. Mark progress; a bar fills at the top of the page.
  3. One reading is “next” and everything else waits behind it.

What it needs

  • Progress bar
  • Queue logic
  • localStorage

Case 05

The Statement Dispenser

Who it's for
Anyone who has to open a presentation and wants ten quiet seconds first.
Why it exists
This page already holds fifteen statements. They should be usable outside it.

How it works

  1. Move the statements into a small JSON file.
  2. Draw one statement in very large type, centred on the screen.
  3. Press space for the next one, F for full screen.

What it needs

  • JSON
  • Keyboard events
  • Fullscreen API
